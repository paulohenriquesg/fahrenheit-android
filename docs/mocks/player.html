The player, grown up (#107)

The details screen answers "should I play this, and from where?". The player answers "where am I, and how do I steer it?". So the player takes the facts you want while listening, and keeps what you use to decide behind panels.

Decided so far: two bars, stacked, one for the chapter (the one you seek with) and a slim one for the whole book. Listening controls (speed, sleep timer). More of what it tells you: the series, the narrator, time left in the chapter and in the book at the current speed.

Frames are 1920×1080 drawn at half size. The cover's colours stand in as a green or plum wash.

A. Side by side

A big cover on the left, and everything you read or press in one column on the right, top to bottom in the order you need it: what this is, where you are, how long is left, then the controls. Focus lands on play. Up reaches the chapter bar, which seeks in 10-second steps (a held key speeds up). Down reaches the action row. The transport keeps five stops, with chapter skip on the outside as frame 4 draws it. The description is behind About rather than always on screen.

A, with About open

About is the decide-what-to-play material, on demand: the description first (focus lands on it, and Down scrolls it a few lines at a time), then the series with the current book marked (choosing another asks before switching), then the facts, one quiet line each, and Mark finished last, kept off the main screen where an accidental press would cost a listener their place. For an episode, About is the show notes and the publish date: no series, no Mark finished.

Getting back to the player: the rail, closed

The player stays full-screen: with the rail beside it, Left from the transport would land in the menu, and the full-width bars of C would lose a fifth of their width. What was missing is the way back. Since #91 playback carries on when you leave the player. So while something plays, the rail carries a Now playing entry above the menu: the cover, a ring for how far through the book you are, and the play state. It is there from every screen, and gone when nothing is queued.
